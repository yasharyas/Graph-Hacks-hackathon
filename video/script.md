# Patient Zero demo script (target 2:28, hard cap 2:30)

Told as the on-call security engineer's story. Record the web app in **replay mode** (`LLM_CHAIN=replay`, so the analyst pick is the recorded one and every take is identical), at the default scenario clocks (qix 13:30 UTC, axios 01:30 UTC). Desktop viewport (1440x900), cursor visible, no browser chrome. Captions are burned in, two lines max, bottom third.

Voiceover rules: calm, first person, no adjectives. Never say "compromised", "infected", "uses" or "installed" about a `WOULD_PULL` repo; say "would have fetched on a fresh install". The line in beat 1 must be read exactly as written. The voiceover is about 320 words, roughly 2:10 at a measured pace, which leaves room for pauses on the clicks.

Every on-screen string below was measured on 2026-10-16 against the production build (`:8080`) and a replay-mode API. Lines marked **(after the round-7 UI merge)** depend on UI work that was not merged when this was written; the recording notes give the fallback.

## Recording notes

- **Setup.** Run the app with `LLM_CHAIN=replay`. The header then reads "Narration mode: LLM replay", and the analyst pick is labelled "ANALYST PICK (LLM, REPLAYED)". A replay is a recorded observation id, not prose: the words are rebuilt from the run's rows.
- **Beat 1.** It needs the autoplay from a fresh load, so reload between takes. To restart the timeline without reloading, use **Play the 90 minutes**; **Pause** (also "Pause the autoplay" in the skip links) stops it. Do not click **Try axios 2026** on the landing: it runs the axios triage at once.
- **Beat 2.** The scrubber is the slider labelled "Scenario time, UTC". Shift+Left and Shift+Right move it 10 minutes; the arrow keys alone move it 1 minute. A manual scrub sets the clock the Run button uses (the autoplay does not), so stop exactly on 13:30: the button must read **Run triage at 13:30 UTC**, the clock that the recorded pick and the memoized cards belong to. At 13:30 the scanner row reads 0, not 1.
- **Beat 3.** Let the first cards land before you open one, so the ms figures are visible. At the default clock the path and fix cards are memoized and say "cached" next to the time. Show it; do not crop it out. **Before the round-7 UI merge**, the analyst pick, the 7-team Notify roster and the Do steps sit behind the disclosure "Show 2 more evidence paths, the analyst pick, who to notify, the steps and the claim map". Open it for this beat.
- **Beat 4.** Use mocha because its path is zero hops and its root pin (`debug@4.3.5`) is safe. Backup if mocha's row is off screen: `github/docs` (`chalk ^5.0.1`, `[E2]`). A card opens with a click on its header line. **Show plan** sits inside the open card, next to **Copy Cypher**, and it turns into **Hide plan**.
- **Beats 5 and 6.** The **Fix** button is the ghost button at the end of the row. The drawer's tabs are **Fix** and **Notice**. **Mark remediated** sits in the drawer's bottom bar, under either tab, and **Undo** replaces it. Esc closes the drawer. **Run again** is in the band above the panes. The mark reaches FalkorDB on that re-run (`POST /api/remediate`), as a `(:Triage)-[:ASSESSED {action: 'remediated'}]->(:Repo)` record scoped to this browser session. The time on screen is the real wall clock in UTC.
- **Beat 7.** The tabs under the exposure table are **Concentration**, **All Cypher used**, **Triage history** and **Graph stats**. Graph stats describes the graph of the run on screen, so this beat must come before axios and the paste.
- **Beat 8.** This beat needs the round-7 Ask routing for named maintainers. The 20 comes from the graph: the `MAINTAINER_BLAST_RADIUS` query for ljharb on `pz_acme_qix` reaches 20 of 21 repos. **Fallback** if that change is not merged: ask "which exposures are prod-only and who owns them?". It reads "Complete list (prod only): 14 repos across 6 teams". Then say "Fourteen repos across six teams, answered from the graph, every name traced" and caption it "14 repos · 6 teams · answered from the graph". Graph answers do not count toward the "0 of 4 model answers" limit.
- **Beat 9.** The radio group in the header is labelled "Scenario": **qix · Sept 2025**, **axios · Mar 2026** and **Describe an alert…**. On screen the verdict says 106 minutes. That runs from the first malicious publish (`axios@1.14.1`, 00:21:58) to the campaign's first OSV record (MAL-2026-2306, for the dropped dependency `plain-crypto-js@4.2.1`, 02:07:58). `axios@1.14.1`'s own record came at 03:15:49, 2 h 54 min after its publish (`docs/proof-points.md`). The video uses 106 only. On this incident the first OSV record (02:07) came before the first vendor alert (02:19), so do not call the vendor first here.
- **Beat 10.** The model picks its own tools. Retake until both `incident_exposure` and `explain_path` show. The server is checked over stdio; see "MCP transcript" in the README.
- **Beat 11.** A paste runs against the incident on screen and gets its own `pz_guest_…` graph. After a paste, a scenario radio re-analyses the pasted repo, which is why the paste comes last. The 9 calls were measured with `mochajs/mocha`. Another public repo with a `package-lock.json` works too, but a repo that reaches no malicious version runs fewer calls, so check the caption. Warm the repo once before the take, and cut the wait if GitHub is slow.
- If a beat runs long, trim the voiceover, not the on-screen action.
